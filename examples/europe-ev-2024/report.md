# Did the European electric passenger-car market shrink in 2024?

> Public display copy: edited and translated from a completed Chinese-language trial, not an original test-run artifact. This is historical research, not a current market assessment.

Period: full-year 2024 versus 2023. Original source access: 21 October 2026. The observations concern annual new passenger-car registrations or sales, not vehicle stock, orders or latent purchase intentions.

## Summary

**Annual electric passenger-car registrations declined in ACEA's EU27 and EU+EFTA+UK coverage; a comprehensive contraction in demand across Europe is not established.** EU27 BEV registrations fell 5.9%; including PHEVs gives a calculated decline of 6.2%. In ACEA's broader region the corresponding declines were 1.3% and 2.1%. Geography and powertrain definitions matter. [ACEA, annual table, p. 4](https://www.acea.auto/files/Press_release_car_registrations_December_2024.pdf).

Countries and buying channels differed. UK BEVs grew 21.4%, while Germany fell 27.4%. IEA described sales in its broader Europe grouping as stagnating. These observations do not establish falling purchases in every country or buyer group, fundamental consumer preference changes, or a single policy cause.

## 1. Which aggregate declined?

All rows below use ACEA's same annual table. Its broader region is EU27 plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and the UK, not all geographical Europe. BEV means battery electric; PHEV means plug-in hybrid. The combined measure excludes conventional hybrids (HEVs) and fuel-cell vehicles.

| Region / powertrain | 2023 registrations | 2024 registrations | Annual change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EU27: BEV | 1,538,106 | 1,447,934 | −5.9% |
| EU27: BEV+PHEV | 2,352,400 | 2,206,878 | −6.2%* |
| EU+EFTA+UK: BEV | 2,018,885 | 1,993,102 | −1.3% |
| EU+EFTA+UK: BEV+PHEV | 3,009,634 | 2,945,160 | −2.1%* |

Source: [ACEA, published 21 January 2025, p. 4, YEAR TO DATE, Jan–Dec 2024/2023, EU and EU+EFTA+UK rows](https://www.acea.auto/files/Press_release_car_registrations_December_2024.pdf). *Calculated by adding BEV and PHEV within each region and year, then applying (2024 / 2023 − 1) × 100%; these are not exact IEA growth figures. PHEVs alone fell 6.8% and 3.9%, respectively.

A supported formulation is that electric new passenger-car registration flows contracted modestly in ACEA's covered European region, with a larger decline in EU27. This does not imply contraction in the entire car market: all-powertrain new registrations grew 0.8% in EU27 and 0.9% in the broader region (same table, Total column).

## 2. Why do other sources say stagnation or record sales?

IEA's Global EV Outlook 2025 describes European electric-car sales as stagnating in 2024, at roughly one-fifth of new sales, similar to 2023. Its electric-car measure includes BEVs and PHEVs. Its Europe grouping additionally includes Turkey, Ukraine and other non-EU markets, including Israel. It cannot replace EU27 BEV registrations or be read as exactly 0.0% growth. [IEA, Europe market discussion](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/trends-in-electric-car-markets-2); [PDF, p. 15 footnote 1 and p. 167 Annex C](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/7ea38b60-3033-42a6-9589-71134f4229f4/GlobalEVOutlook2025.pdf).

Definitions and the qualitative wording explain why these statements need not contradict each other. However, the trial did not obtain and reaggregate the complete same-version IEA annual quantities: **the full numerical difference remains unreconciled**.

UK BEVs rose from 314,687 to 381,970 (+21.4%); German BEVs fell 27.4%, while Norway and Belgium grew 9.4% and 36.9%. These counterexamples reject universal national decline, but do not overturn a volume-weighted regional decline. National growth rates must not be averaged to infer regional totals. [ACEA p. 4](https://www.acea.auto/files/Press_release_car_registrations_December_2024.pdf); [SMMT annual fuel-type table](https://www.smmt.co.uk/record-ev-market-share-but-weak-private-demand-frustrates-ambition/).

An industry article reports 381,907 UK BEVs, 63 fewer than the matching ACEA and SMMT value of 381,970. This report uses the consistent original statistics; the reason for the discrepancy was not confirmed with the author. [electrive, 21 January 2025, UK discussion](https://www.electrive.com/2025/01/21/more-new-electric-cars-than-diesel-registered-across-the-eu/).

## 3. What can be said about demand?

Realised electric new-car purchase/registration flows weakened in some regions, with divergent national performance. SMMT reports around 64,000 additional UK business/fleet BEV purchases and describes private demand as weak, alongside record total BEV registrations. [SMMT, private and business/fleet discussion](https://www.smmt.co.uk/record-ev-market-share-but-weak-private-demand-frustrates-ambition/). [EAFO's UK article, EV Market Trends / Barriers to Growth](https://alternative-fuels-observatory.ec.europa.eu/general-information/news/united-kingdom-becomes-largest-bev-market-europe-196-market-share-2024) repeats SMMT, so it is not a second independent verification.

The following conclusions remain unsupported:

- **UK private BEV registrations fell 8.7%.** That decline concerns all-powertrain private registrations. Around one in ten private buyers choosing BEV is a 2024 share, not a year-on-year change. A complete private-by-BEV two-year cross-tab is missing (SMMT annual sales-type table and private-buyer discussion).
- **Consumer preferences retreated universally across Europe.** Prices, taxes, subsidies, supply and channels are not controlled; comparable Europe-wide private purchase and intention data are missing, and national counterexamples exist.
- **German subsidy withdrawal was the sole cause, or its causal contribution is quantified.** IEA links reduced policy support in some large markets and the emissions-target schedule to stagnation; this is an institutional explanation, not causal identification or a decomposition of contributions (IEA Europe policy discussion above).
- **One annual decline proves a long-term trend.** Only two full years were compared. No forecast or investment advice is provided.

## 4. Method and remaining limits

The trial used five main public materials: ACEA and SMMT statistics, IEA analysis, electrive reporting and EAFO news. The latter two depend on ACEA and SMMT; IEA also draws partly on shared underlying data. Counting sources is not independent corroboration. Arithmetic can be reproduced from the saved annual inputs in [data.json](data.json).

“Full-year 2024 public materials” means materials covering 2024, including summaries published in 2025, not information available by the end of 2024. ACEA and electrive were published on 21 January 2025, SMMT on 6 January, EAFO on 15 January. IEA was published in May 2025; the PDF read was marked revised July 2025. Earlier versions were not compared. Reading was limited to relevant tables, methodological appendices and passages, not every page.

Open issues are the exact IEA–ACEA quantity bridge, Europe-wide channel/powertrain cross-tabs, causal identification, and ACEA–SMMT HEV classification differences. HEVs are excluded from the electric-car totals. These limits constrain extrapolation to all European demand, while preserving the bounded BEV/PHEV registration finding. The saved-source evidence was not fully re-fetched for this public edition; arithmetic consistency does not independently authenticate the original tables.
